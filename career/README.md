## What changed in the CV, and why

| Before | After | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Senior Project Manager with 14 years delivering complex technology programs" | "AI leader with 14 years in regulated financial services — now building and leading AI capability end to end" | The first line decides whether a Head of AI screener keeps reading. Delivery credibility is the *support*, not the headline. |
| SAP S/4HANA testing as the lead bullet under Capgemini | AI enablement rollout, use-case prioritisation model and evaluation framework lead; S/4HANA last | For an AI role the S/4HANA work is background, not the story. |
| AI products as a flat list at the bottom | A framed portfolio table, each entry expressed as AI *leadership* evidence (governance position, evaluation, risk design) | Recruiters read "7 side projects." The reframe reads "this person has shipped regulated AI." |
| "Hard Skills" as one long alphabetical string | Five capability blocks matching how Head of AI JDs are written: Strategy & Commercial, Governance & Risk, LLM & Agentic, Cost & FinOps, Delivery & People | Makes the ATS keyword match *and* the human scan work. |
| No commercial framing | AML tuning, Kandoo, portfolio invoicing and savings figures surfaced as commercial evidence | Head of AI JDs increasingly carry a P&L and BD expectation. |
| Job titles verbatim | "AI Delivery & Enablement Lead (Consultant)" for the current role | Accurate to what you actually do and closer to the target title. Verify it's acceptable to state before using. |

**Kept honest:** every metric, employer, date and claim comes from your existing CV. Nothing was invented.
The gaps — no formal P&L, no external sales quota — are named and addressed with scripted answers in the
prep doc rather than papered over.

## About Project AEGIS

`case-study-aegis/` is a **reference case study and blueprint**, not a record of a completed engagement. The
frameworks are real and reusable — the EU AI Act conformity mapping, the risk register, the intake scoring
model, the token-level cost model, the practice P&L. The financial and performance figures are illustrative
targets modelled on realistic mid-market European bank parameters, and every document says so.

Use it three ways:

1. **Interview structure.** It gives you a complete, coherent answer to "walk me through how you'd build an
   AI function" — with numbers and artefacts rather than adjectives.
2. **A build target.** Most of it is buildable with your existing stack. RegCopilot already proves the
   retrieval and audit-trail half; ComplianceIQ proves the triage half.
3. **A credibility asset.** Publishing the method — conformity mapping, cost model, risk register — is the
   cheapest inbound pipeline a new practice has, and it is the specific BD motion recommended in the
   commercial doc.

**Do not present it as delivered client work.** Present it as your operating model: *"this is how I run an
AI function, and here's the full model."* That is a stronger claim than a case study anyone can ask
references about.
