## 3. The gaps, honestly — and how to close them

### Gap 1: no formal P&L ownership *(the biggest one)*

**Do not overclaim it.** Interviewers in this space test it, and a fabricated P&L claim ends the process.

**Say this instead:**

> "I haven't held a signed P&L. What I have owned is every input to one — estimation, resourcing, portfolio
> invoicing, vendor spend, budget management, and the business case for the work. And I've built the P&L I'd
> run: revenue model, blended rate, utilisation, margin levers, hiring ladder, and what I'd commit to in year
> one — including that year one is a small planned loss and why. I'd rather show you that than claim a title."

Then show Part B of the case study. **Bringing a modelled practice P&L to the interview is a stronger signal
than having held one badly.**

### Gap 2: no external sales quota

**Say this:**

> "My BD experience is internal — I built the AI demand pipeline at Capgemini from nothing and tripled pilot
> proposals in three months, which is the same motion: find the problem, build the case, get it funded. What
> I've also done is build the asset that generates external inbound. My published AI products and case
> studies are how a regulated buyer decides I've done this before, and that's the cheapest pipeline a new
> unit has."

Then name your pipeline model: 45 conversations → 8 assessments → 4 builds → 2 retainers, and the wedge offer
(the €38–55k AI Governance Readiness Assessment). **Having a specific first offer with a price is what
separates a plausible answer from a credible one.**

### Gap 3: the AI products are recent and self-built, not client-delivered at scale

**Reframe, don't defend:**

> "They're deliberately small and complete rather than large and partial. Each one has a named user, a
> measurable outcome, an evaluation framework and a compliance position — because the thing I wanted to prove
> isn't that I can build a demo, it's that I know what production means in a regulated context. RegCopilot
> scores 82% on a golden set and I can tell you exactly why the AMLD6 corpus is the weak one and what v2
> does about it. That's the conversation I want to have with your engineers."

### Gap 4: German at B1 for a DACH market-facing role

Address it before they do: B1 and improving, English is the working language of the buyers you'd target
(group-level, CRO/CTO), and you have been in Stuttgart since 2023. If the role is genuinely German-language
client-facing, ask directly in the first conversation rather than discovering it at offer stage.

## 4. Your 90-day plan for the interview

Every interview for this role will ask some version of "what would you do first." Have this ready:

**Days 1–30 — Facts before strategy**
- Map the group's existing client base and identify the 10 warmest Data & AI conversations
- Audit existing delivery assets, credentials and case studies that can be repurposed
- Interview 15 people: group leadership, adjacent venture leads, any existing client contacts
- Define the initial value proposition and the first two offers — priced

**Days 31–60 — First proof**
- Take the wedge offer (AI Governance Readiness Assessment) to market with three warm accounts
- Publish two method artefacts (conformity mapping, cost model) to seed inbound
- Open the first hire: AI Risk & Governance Lead
- Stand up the delivery asset base — evaluation harness, conformity pack template, risk-register library

**Days 61–90 — First revenue and a plan**
- Close the first assessment engagement
- Build the year-1 business plan with pipeline model, P&L and hiring ladder — presented, not assumed
- Second hire opened
- One published case study in progress

**The line to end on:** *"I'd rather have one signed assessment and a real pipeline model at day 90 than a
polished strategy deck. The strategy is easier to write once something is sold."*

## 5. Likely hard questions

| Question | Your line |
|---|---|
| "You've never carried a P&L." | §3 Gap 1 verbatim. Then show the modelled practice P&L. |
| "How would you win the first client with no reference base?" | Wedge offer at €38–55k, warm group introductions first, published method artefacts as credibility, sell to Risk as well as the business. |
| "How technical are you really?" | Pick one: the AEGIS routing cascade and why cost-per-resolved-alert beats cost-per-token, or why RAG over fine-tuning for citation-grounded regulatory answers. **Go deep on one, don't survey.** |
| "Where does AI actually fail in a bank?" | It doesn't fail technically. It fails at the second-line gate, because nobody produced the evidence. Then: governance before architecture, and why AEGIS was scoped as assistive on purpose. |
| "What's the biggest risk in an agentic system?" | Not hallucination — automation bias. If the human oversight is a rubber stamp, the compliance position is false. Then explain the override-rate *floor* and blind controls. |
| "How do you control AI cost?" | Cost per outcome, not per token. Caching, routing cascade, batching, context budgeting → 63% reduction. Then the punchline: inference was 0.3% of programme cost; the money is in data access and conformity. |
| "Why should we hire a project manager as Head of AI?" | "Because the constraint on AI in regulated markets isn't model capability — it's getting from demo to signed-off production, and that's a delivery, governance and stakeholder problem. I've done that for 14 years, and I've now built the AI systems too. The rarer half of that pair is the first one." |
| "What would you not do?" | Name it: no healthcare or public sector in year 1; no high-risk-classified use case as the first build; no benefit claim I can't measure. **Being able to say what you'd decline is a seniority signal.** |
